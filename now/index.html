The verdict in 60 seconds

The format is booming right now. Uploads in this format went from 4 per quarter in late 2024 to 55 in the current quarter. Videos posted in the last 6 months are pulling a median of 1,672 views per day against 859 for videos 1–2 years old. This is not a settled niche — it is mid-explosion.

The single biggest lever is WHAT you do, and the answer is mischief. Ranked by views per day: action / crime activities 9,374, build & tycoon 2,212, survival 1,689, facts 991, and cozy farming games dead last on 104. That is a 90× gap between “Fall Asleep While I Rob Rich People” and “fall asleep while I tend my farm”.

The channel to study is sleepyslorgs. 154,000 subs, every single one of its 24 videos posted in the last 12 months, median 351,859 views. Its newest — Fall Asleep While I Operate Heavy Artillery — did 602,431 views in 5 days = 120,486 views per day, the fastest-moving video in the whole dataset.

And a brand-new channel just proved it is still open. Kindly Sleepin’ went from 17 subscribers to 17,000 in about a week off one video: Fall Asleep While I Satisfyingly Clean Extremely Broken Game Consoles.

01 · Method

What this report counts

Only content where a person talks. Every music, ambience, rain, C418 and no-commentary longplay video was filtered out — 477 genuinely voiced sleep videos survived, across 123 channels. Real upload dates were then pulled per video so everything could be ranked by views per day since upload, which is the only fair way to compare a video posted last week with one posted in 2024.

Why views per day Raw view counts reward old videos. A 2024 video with 3.4M views has had 600 days to get there; a video with 600,000 views in 5 days is moving 22× faster. Everything in this report is ranked the second way, because the question was what is working now.
The honest limits Upload dates resolved for 139 of 477 videos before rate limiting — the rest still count in the corpus totals but not in the recency tables. Every recency figure below therefore rests on 139 dated videos plus an independently-gathered 154-video set, which agree with each other. Sub-genre classification is by title keyword. Where a number could not be pulled it is marked n/a rather than guessed.

08 · Two people

Does a duo work in this format?

The format is overwhelmingly solo, but there is a real two-person data point and it is encouraging.

MadMorph’s solo median over the same window is around 912 views/day. The collabs land inside and above that range — and they are current (Nov 2025 to Mar 2026), not historic. Two voices is not a penalty in this format.
But open-ended chatting is the weak part — and this is the warning for you Down To Sleep’s own “Ask Me Anything” video did 35 views/day against 284 and 1,084 for that same channel’s structured story narrations — it underperformed its own channel by 8× to 31×. Across the whole sweep, the “we just chat / Q&A / life update” style has no example working at scale. The two-person content that performs is built around doing a thing. “Two friends talking” is the part the data argues against; “two friends doing something ridiculous and talking while they do it” is the part it supports.
What is still unproven No channel is built around two people in this format — the duo evidence is collaborations between established solo channels, not a native duo, and it could not be confirmed by listening whether those videos are true two-way banter throughout or a host plus a guest. So: two people demonstrably does not break the format, but nobody has made it a channel’s whole identity yet. That is the opening.
The production rule that decides it Sleep content works because the noise floor is steady. Two friends bantering is the one thing that naturally spikes. Record on separate tracks, compress and hard-limit so a laugh is audible but never louder, and never both raise your voice at once. Get that right and “Fall Asleep While We…” is an untaken position in a format that is currently exploding.

09 · Do this

The brief

The formula, straight off the data
  1. Title: Fall Asleep While We [slightly-wrong verb] [absurd object]. Same prefix every single upload.
  2. Activity: action, crime, chaos or building something ridiculous. Not cozy farming — that bucket runs at 104 views/day.
  3. Length: 2 to 2 hours 30. Not 45 minutes. Not 5 hours.
  4. Delivery: calm, quiet, steady. The premise carries the comedy; your voice stays flat.
  5. Cadence: sleepyslorgs put out 24 in twelve months — roughly one every fortnight — and that was enough to build the whole channel.

Ten titles that fit the winning pattern

  1. Fall Asleep While We Run the Worst Business in the City
  2. Fall Asleep While We Commit Increasingly Petty Crimes
  3. Fall Asleep While We Build a Village and Ruin Each Other’s Reputations
  4. Fall Asleep While We Take Over an Island We Are Not Qualified to Run
  5. Fall Asleep While We Steal Everything That Isn’t Nailed Down
  6. Fall Asleep While We Build the Most Unethical Zoo Possible
  7. Fall Asleep While We Terrorise a Perfectly Nice Village
  8. Fall Asleep While We Clean Up the Mess We Made Last Video
  9. Fall Asleep While We Argue About Something That Doesn’t Matter
  10. Fall Asleep While We Slowly Destroy the Housing Market
Where your original ideas fit “Our worst rizz attempts” is a talking premise, and the talking-led lane is thinner than the doing-led one — the data here is dominated by people doing something. The strongest version merges the two: do something ridiculous on screen and tell the stories while you do it. Fall Asleep While We Build a Village and Tell You Our Worst Rizz Attempts gets the activity hook that the algorithm rewards and keeps the content you actually want to make.
The one number to remember 9× more people are uploading this format than a year ago, and videos posted in the last six months are outperforming everything older. Both trends are steep. The window is open and it is visibly closing — Kindly Sleepin’ went 17 to 17,000 subscribers in a week, in August, doing exactly this.
